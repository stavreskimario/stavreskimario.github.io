# Concourse enclosure, floor lights and furnishing detail

This ledger concerns the next architectural finish pass. It distinguishes documented construction from modelled dimensions. A source-layer geometry audit or fresh Blender export is not proof that the resulting compressed assets, lighting and browser experience have passed final review.

## Primary evidence inspected

- George Gibbs, *The New York Tunnel Extension of the Pennsylvania Railroad: Station Construction, Road, Track, Yard Equipment, Electric Traction, and Locomotives*, ASCE 69 (1910), pp. 254–256 and 267. [Original paper](https://ascelibrary.org/doi/10.1061/TACEAT.0002223), [original-volume scan](https://archive.org/details/transactionsofam69amer). The freshly retrieved original-volume OCR was checked against the existing research ledger. Fig. 2 on printed p. 243 (original scan PDF page 427) registers the concourse, cloisters and stair heads; it does not draw a prism-panel schedule. No later photographic restoration is substituted as a construction source.
- Detroit Publishing photograph 071935, [LOC 2016815390](https://www.loc.gov/item/2016815390/), digital file `det.4a23718`. LOC dates it broadly 1905–1915 and reports no known publication restrictions. The photo resolves the east-wall composition, lower gridded openings, separate arched lights, office sign and square floor-light fields. It does not establish a complete 1935 inventory.
- Ezzes, 13 March 1936 interior, [NYC Municipal Archives' original dated photograph and catalog context](https://www.archives.nyc/blog/2024/6/7/re-discovering-the-old-pennsylvania-station). Its west-wall view corroborates rectangular grid bodies, high arched transoms, opaque intervening stone, the raised west doorway and a clock. The online reference remains research-only; it is not bundled as an image asset without separate rights clearance.
- Detroit Publishing photograph 071928, [LOC 2016815386](https://www.loc.gov/item/2016815386/), digital original `det.4a23713`. The original 5076×4061 scan was retrieved again and matches the already retained source SHA256. This supplies ticket-booth/frieze/base-vent vocabulary, floor-lamp details and the actual north mural recovery.

## Geometry and review boundary

The earlier revision 2 isolated layer checked 154 closed surfaces, shared data for 320 glass/concrete panels and 186 axial-passage rays. Its 320 panels covered only 460.8 m². That floor coverage is superseded by revision 3; the older reports are retained as development evidence.

The final revision 3 raw export passes 7,464 glass-support rays, 2,488 concrete-web rays, 7,464 checks for unwanted opaque slab beneath the prisms, 180 supports across all 20 retained bearing cells, 54 supports across six actual pier footprints, 840 field/concrete-boundary supports, 81 stair-tread rays and 186 axial-passage rays, with zero failures. Stair/level/access metadata equals frozen revision 2. The combined source bearing audit reports 2,490 contacts and zero failures. These are scoped geometry checks before final lighting, compression and browser acceptance.

The broad floor is registered at world X[-95.4,-49.8] m and Z[-39.6,39.6] m. It fills the modelled visible central open court rather than two representative patches. The plan and photograph justify that architectural scope; they do not survey exact panel edges or pitch. Only active panel rectangles cut the 0.44 m Main slab. Omitted cells beneath existing pier baseplates remain full-thickness concrete, preventing unsupported bases or holes at the exclusions. The source retains all existing stair openings and circulation routes.

Repeated fine webs use `ConcourseReinforcedVaultLightConcrete`; retained large floor/bearing slabs use `ConcourseOpaqueCirculationConcrete` with identical optical values. The distinct names allow separate lighting-atlas treatment without dropping large opaque surfaces from their baked illumination. The final raw material-reference patch preserves the geometry BIN exactly; receipts and actual-triangle checks are archived under `authoring/penn-station-1935/review/phase4/concourse-details/floor-v3/`.

The repeated prism and concrete panels share two master meshes. A scoped `preserve_linked_geometry` flag prevents the static batching step from flattening these repetitions. Concrete web strips own their intersections, avoiding duplicate coplanar top faces. Window bodies, glass heads and opaque spandrels are physically separate, with closed panes and explicit outward winding on either wall axis.

The source preserves established roof spans, pier coordinates, stair heads, access centres, floor datums and the maintenance checkpoint's moved indicator posts. Adding transparent floor glass requires an explicit upward-support rule in browser walking; ordinary vertical glass must not become a floor. New sources, bakes, compression, source geometry and actual browser navigation still require coordinated final acceptance before publication.
