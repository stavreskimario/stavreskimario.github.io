## Supports, floors and enclosing roofs

**North/south waiting-room screens.** S1 printed p. 11 explicitly describes foot-passenger bridges at street level crossing the carriage driveways into the waiting room. S2 plate 302 and S23 show the raised end landings and their descending stairs. The six Ionic columns stand on a continuous raised platform, with a central stair to Main and enclosed accommodation/doors beneath the flanks. A column floating above the low hall floor is not supported by this evidence.

Plate 300 supplies a measurable north-end stair footprint. On the 1681×2500 research rendering, the approximately 200 ft scale-bar interval spans X587–1013. The flight occupies approximately X1019–1094 and Y1154–1192: about **35 ft run ×18 ft clear width (10.7×5.5 m)**, with roughly ±2 ft raster uncertainty. This is a plan-scaled estimate, not a printed dimension. A short landing separates the upper tread from the Ionic screen. Do not infer a 12 m flight merely by subtracting the published 277 ft clear-room and 314 ft 4 in room extents. World endpoints require the modeller's common plan registration.

**Concourse bearings.** S22 shows each ornamental arcade meeting an actual pier collar or adjoining structural member. Its lattice piers continue to floor bases; riveted/plated upper joints connect the main arches. Horizontal lattice transoms and triangular spandrel bracing connect the glazed perimeter to the lower enclosing wall. S9 printed pp. 197–199 independently describes double-latticed ornamental arches with two webs, upper binding struts and the lower Guastavino/steel vaults. S24 supplies a 1930s check of pier feet and stair/bridge edges. These are connection references, not a complete fabrication schedule.

**Eastern roof distinction.** The two train-shed roof footprints run east–west, flanking the arcade. S9 printed pp. 193–194 gives each hipped roof as 152 ft 8½ in by 115 ft 6 in, its monitor as 91 ft 7½ in by 20 ft 4 in, and adjacent depressed strips as 17 ft wide. S10 printed p. 240 identifies these as open courts with glass skylights near street level, above tracks. S25 confirms the four-storey inner walls remain exposed above them. Missing upper office/arcade roof planes must be restored without inventing tall glass roofs across those genuinely open courts. S9 p. 188 gives average facade heights around 65 ft above curb and entrance pavilions around 75 ft; local roof and cornice datums still need tracing.

## One actual mural recovered

The approved asset is [mural-north-archival.png](../assets/mural-north-archival.png), assigned **only to GuerinMuralField6, the north end**. The north orientation is inferred from the east arcade/grand stair appearing on the photograph's right and the registered room plan. This is a photograph of the actual panel in place, not a newly drawn railway map. It visibly contains the Americas, Atlantic, Europe and Africa; an official panel title has not been verified.

The source-preserving PNG is 728×260 pixels (70:25 aspect), 76,559 bytes. It mechanically rectifies S23's painted field using source-image corners UL(2166,2022), LL(2165,2284), LR(2894,2273), UR(2897,2006). No geography, text or colour was generated, retouched or inpainted. Exposure falloff, dust and photographic defects remain. A separate global-contrast inspection derivative stays outside the app and is not a reconstruction of the original palette.

Credit: Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division, Detroit Publishing Company Collection; LC-DIG-det-4a23713. The original pre-1931 photograph is released with no known publication restrictions. Original scan and output hashes, dimensions and treatment are recorded in repository-root `authoring/penn-station-1935/references/phase3/mural-north-provenance.json`.

S27, the [1911 Copyright Office catalog](https://archive.org/details/catalogofcopyrig64libr), printed p. 80, independently lists Detroit negative 071928 with copyright date 16 January 1911, registration J151601 and deposited copies on 6 February 1911. This strengthens the original-publication evidence; it does not supply the exposure day or the mural's original colour.

**Limits:** this is a low-resolution monochrome recovery suitable for a distant archival reading of one panel. It does not establish original colours or recover fine lettering. It must not be repeated on the other five panels. S16 names Lines West of Pittsburgh and The Lines on Long Island as two subjects, but their wall assignments are still unverified. S18's scaffold photograph remains partial evidence. The other five fields stay neutral until their own imagery and placements are supported.

## Remaining primary-source conflict

Francis and O'Brien printed p. 194 gives 143 ft to the highest waiting-room ceiling and 167 ft to the roof. Gibbs's table p. 252 and construction account p. 257 give 150 ft; the latter explicitly calls it floor to ceiling. Thus 150 ft is supported by an engineering account as well as PRR descriptions, and cannot be dismissed solely as promotional floor-to-roof wording. Section registration and finished intrados profiles must resolve the difference. Keep both statements visible until then.

### One Eric proof, withheld from the exhibition

The experimental `build_scanned_passenger.py`, retained outside the repository in `/private/tmp/penn-phase3-passenger-experiments/`, imports only the licensed FBX and original maps. It is not delivered app source. It uses the original skeleton to lower the arms, relax the wrists and flex the fingers, preserves photographed clothing and RGB/normal textures, and assigns skin/eyes/hair/cloth/leather materials from the original masks and UV islands. Roughness and skin scattering remain artist estimates. The original editable rig is saved; the GLB contains its evaluated static pose because unanimated skin export otherwise restored the original A-pose. The actual browser check caught and corrected that discrepancy.

Proof outputs remain outside the app in `/private/tmp/penn-phase3-scanned-passenger/`: `eric-scanned-passenger-editable.blend`, `eric-scanned-passenger.glb`, `source-and-review.json`, six `cycles-*.png` views, four `webgl-*.png` views and `webgl-review.json`. RGB and normal maps retain their original 8192×8192 resolution; the mechanically derived roughness map is 2048×2048.

Browser review uses the actual exhibition's `sky.hdr`, afternoon sun position/intensity/colour, environment intensity 0.75, exposure 1.04 and ACES colour mapping. Full-body front/side views are about 3.3–3.4 m from the figure; face images deliberately expose close-view limits. It is a neutral-ground exterior-lighting test, not proof of integration with interior occlusion or baked station lighting. Chrome Headless 141.0.7390.37 loaded all seven material groups without page errors and retained the narrow relaxed-pose bounds rather than the 1.31 m A-pose width.

**Rejected for the current release after parent review.** The scan is a substantial improvement over the rejected MakeHuman figures, but moulded-looking hair, stiff separated fingers, eye response in strong sunlight and contemporary low-waist/slim trousers with a short waistcoat fail the current quality/period threshold. No cloning or promotion is authorized for this proof. Realistic period passengers remain outstanding. It is not certified as near-photographic at face distance or as authentic 1936 dress. ### Clothing evidence and limits

The Met's surviving American wool suits [1979.59.14a–c, dated 1935](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/92046) and [1979.59.15a–c, dated 1937](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/92047) are primary garment references. Their restricted photographs were not downloaded or bundled, and the catalog dates alone do not establish Eric's fit.

Two contemporary published sources give a concrete reason to question the scan's narrow trouser silhouette. The **Big Spring Daily Herald, 1 October 1936, p. 5**, Burr's advertisement, explicitly promotes full trousers, alongside single- and double-breasted coats ([university-hosted original page](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/bitstreams/581ce898-b9f5-408f-b23e-5b2fddfec828/download)). The **Ranger Times, 20 December 1936, p. 2**, fashion column, identifies pleated trousers and suspenders as current styles ([original page](https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/bitstreams/1d602068-f4b5-4932-b1a6-3e5eaf4399d8/download)). These are American period evidence, not a uniform dress rule for all Penn Station passengers; neither proves that every narrow tie or belted trouser was impossible. No quantitative late-1930s tie-width source has been authenticated for this proof. Keep the modern fit explicitly unverified rather than broadening or relabelling it without evidence. These newspaper and museum references are link-only, not packaged artwork.

### Independent corrective render review

The 29 October 2026 review compared assembled views with plate 301, plate 302, the opening-era Bain roof photograph and the authenticated interior photographs. It identified the central Seventh Avenue clock attic's incorrect 6.7 m frontage; the corrected roughly 30.2 m band now spans the central pavilion as the primary elevation shows. It also found a hanging source column, black window/roof ribbons, exterior lantern corner seams and a black end-wall band. The modeller traced the latter defects to stale transforms and duplicate coplanar surfaces rather than verified architectural openings.

The corrected exterior `/private/tmp/penn-phase3-architecture/review-final/exterior-southeast.png` and hall `/private/tmp/penn-phase3-architecture/review-hall-final/waiting-floor.png` were independently rechecked. The conspicuous column and black seams/bands are absent; the wall below the end lunette is continuous stone. The accompanying architectural audit reports 2,334 actual-mesh contacts and zero failures. This closes the specifically identified corrective enclosure/attachment gate in the reviewed views, not a complete geometry or photorealism certification.

At the Phase3 review, the lower concourse perimeter still omitted the substantial gridded/arched fenestration and documented prism-light construction. Those omissions belong to that reviewed release; the later source-detail pass below has separate evidence and requires its own delivered-asset/browser acceptance.
