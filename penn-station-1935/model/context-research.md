# Immediate Manhattan context — evidence and authoring notes

This extension adds recognisable neighbours to the Penn Station study: the **Hotel Pennsylvania on the east side of Seventh Avenue between West 32nd and 33rd Streets**, and the **General Post Office, now Farley, west of Eighth Avenue**. It also adds original vehicle, street-standard and pavement assets. It does not certify a complete 1935 streetscape, every neighbouring parcel, or a photographic match.

## Primary evidence used

- **The Architectural Forum, 1919, “Hotel Pennsylvania, New York City,” printed pp. 93–104.** [Period article scan](https://www.smpub.com/ubb/images/18/26-2716-architectural_forum.pdf). The text records the 400 × 200 ft lot, four continuous lower floors, seventeen bedroom floors, four upper wings each 54 ft wide, and three courts opening south, each 40 ft wide. It distinguishes Indiana limestone below and buff brick above. Some endpoints reject automated retrieval; the independently indexed article text and the separate published plan below were checked.
- **Hotel Monthly, volume 27, number 321, December 1919, p. 51, typical guest-floor plan.** [Original plan and publication record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hotel_Pennsylvania_typical_floor_plan.jpg). Its caption and room/court layout were visually inspected. It establishes the comb of four southern fingers, the continuous central corridor, and the smaller northern courts. The plan's original publication predates 1931; no downloaded reference raster is embedded in this asset.
- **Record and Guide, 25 January 1919, printed p. 105.** [Columbia University scan](https://rerecord.library.columbia.edu/pdf_files/ldpd_7031148_063_30.pdf). Records the hotel blockfront from 32nd to 33rd, six Ionic entrance columns, the 15 ft setback from the Seventh Avenue house line, a 400 × 200 ft lot, and twenty-two floors from street to roof with three further penthouse levels. Reported lot dimensions are rounded period descriptions, not a modern boundary survey.
- **McKim, Mead & White, monograph volume IV, plates 360–364, 1913 General Post Office.** [Original plates, Getty Research Institute / Internet Archive scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b3/A_monograph_of_the_work_of_McKim%2C_Mead_%26_White%2C_1879-1915_.._%28IA_gri_33125006526038%29.pdf). The full facade photograph, front elevation/plan, capital photograph and exterior detail sheet were inspected. They support the raised east colonnade, fluted Corinthian order, metal window lattice, windowed attic, carved crest, square terminal pavilions with low stepped pyramid caps, and side pilasters. Plate 364 specifies granite. The surviving original is public domain in the United States; its raster is not packaged as a material.
- **NYC Landmarks Preservation Commission, LP-0232, 17 May 1966.** [Designation report](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/0232.pdf). Specifies twenty Corinthian columns 53 ft high and thirty-one steps reaching the entrance 22 ft above street level. This is a primary government designation record describing the building, rather than an original construction survey.
- **ASCE Transactions LXIX (1910), Penn Station engineering account, printed p. 188.** [Full period volume](https://archive.org/details/transactionsofam69amer). The paragraph beginning “The Station Building covers the site…” gives approximately 19 ft from the station's Seventh Avenue facade to the house line, 7 ft at Eighth Avenue and 13 ft at 31st/33rd. Its approximate 775 × 430 ft building extents differ from the other primary publications; this extension does not silently replace the parent model's nominal 784 × 430 ft anchors.
- **NYC Planning, 15 Penn Plaza FEIS, chapter 8, 2010, pp. 8-3–8-5.** [City planning record](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/download/pdf/applicants/env-review/15_penn/08_feis.pdf). Corroborates the hotel's location, original six Ionic columns and the later cutting of four columns for a marquee. The new context keeps six full-height columns and does not reproduce that later alteration. The source identifies major post-1935 neighbourhood changes; its present-day map is not treated as a 1935 map.

The early hotel exterior photo found through Wikimedia was used only as a scratch visual reference. Its uploader-level public-domain declaration does not establish the original photographer/publication chain, so the photo is **not** part of this deliverable's reusable media.

**Hotel dimensional reconciliation:** the primary Architectural Forum text on printed p. 96 identifies the easterly 50 ft of the ground floor beneath an eastern court as parallel service drives. The four 54 ft fingers plus three 40 ft courts total 336 ft; adding the stated 15 ft western setback and this eastern 50 ft allowance gives 401 ft, approximately the rounded 400 ft lot description. The model retains the 336 ft upper comb, 15 ft western setback and a 49 ft lower eastern allowance. This leaves the one-foot publication/rounding closure visible as an inference; it is not a measured survey. The guest-floor plan should not be stretched to fill the full 400 ft ground-floor lot.

## Geometry and confidence

| Feature | Implemented | Confidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hotel location | East Seventh Avenue, north side of West 32nd, extending to West 33rd | Block identity documented. World registration remains a working inference. |
| Hotel lower floors | One continuous stone base over the period lot | Four-floor continuity and material documented. The 62 ft base height is an interpreted working height, not certified from the primary plan. |
| Hotel upper plan | Four 54 ft wings, three 40 ft south-open courts; northern courts and corridor proportions read from the original guest plan | Principal dimensions documented. The 336 ft comb fits over a longer 400 ft lower lot; do not stretch the upper fingers to fill the entire base. Small northern-court dimensions are raster-derived. |
| Hotel upper elevation | Seventeen repeated guest floors, four-storey attic articulation, projecting copper cornice, six Ionic entry columns | Floor count and order documented. Floor height 3.4 m, individual window/bay spacing, column profiles and penthouse silhouette interpreted. No invented roof advertisement or business sign. |
| Farley principal order | Twenty fluted Corinthian columns, 53 ft height; 31 solid stone stair treads rising 22 ft | Count, order, height, steps and rise documented. Column diameter, tread run, intercolumniation, capital carving and entablature dimensions interpreted from the plates. |
| Farley envelope | Working 355 × 332 ft original body; two corner pavilions, attic and side wings | **Not an authenticated measured footprint.** Modern summaries and period postcards give conflicting extents. A full scaled registration of foldout plate 362 is outstanding. |
| Farley rear | A quieter west annex volume, present for the circa-1935 target | Existence/dating documented by the archival research. Its 104 m depth, windows and height are provisional, not a facade-by-facade reconstruction. |
| Farley workroom roof | Low repeated rooflights over a central mail room | Workroom arrangement is plan-led; exact historic rooflight profiles remain unverified. No modern Moynihan roof is represented as historical evidence. |
| Vehicles | Closed sedan and taxi-type saloon, six linked placements | Original generic 1930s interpretations, **not authenticated makes, model years, taxi livery or parked positions**. Rounded coachwork, separate wheel/fender geometry, split glazing, hood louvres, radiator slats, wire spokes and lamps are modelled. |
| Street standards | Twenty-two linked cast-iron curled-arm standards | Plausible period product interpretation. Exact local fixture type, height and locations are not verified. |
| Street surfaces | Stone slabs, granite curbs, drains; no modern zebra crossings or road paint | Working material and width interpretation. Exact 1935 paving, curb heights and furniture inventory require dated street photographs/maps. |

Farley's reserved sculpture panels have the documented broad positions and outlines, while their federal reliefs remain unresolved. They are not filled with invented generic figures.

## Registration and street datum

All authored coordinates are metres. Blender uses X east, Y north, Z up; export maps these to glTF X east, Y up, Z south. Street/sidewalk grade is 0. Carriageways are at −0.15 m; tyres meet that surface. This asset must **not** be shifted with the station's lower passenger-floor datum.

The current station facade half-extents are 119.4816 m east–west and 65.532 m north–south. A facade extent is not a property/house line. Applying the ASCE setbacks as working offsets gives:

| Working control | Coordinate |
| --- | ---: |
| Seventh Avenue station-side house line | X +125.2728 m |
| Eighth Avenue station-side house line | X −121.6152 m |
| North/south house lines | glTF Z ±69.4944 m |
| Hotel-side Seventh Avenue house line, assuming 100 ft avenue | X +155.7528 m |
| Hotel west wall after its stated 15 ft setback | X +160.3248 m |
| Hotel southern house line beside a 60 ft West 32nd | glTF Z −9.144 m |
| Hotel northern working edge using the stated 200 ft lot | glTF Z −70.104 m |
| Farley east stair foot, assuming 100 ft Eighth Avenue | X −152.0952 m |

The hotel northern edge differs from the inferred station-side north house line by 0.6096 m. This exposes the incompatible rounded dimensions rather than concealing them. The 100 ft avenue, 60 ft side street, 20 ft avenue sidewalk and 10 ft cross-street sidewalk are declared working grid values pending a registered, date-checked map. **No survey-accuracy claim is supported by these coordinates.** West 32nd's carriageway is authored only east of Seventh Avenue; it does not cut through the station.

## Reproduction and optimisation

Run from the repository root:

```sh
UV_CACHE_DIR=/private/tmp/penn-uv-cache uv run --no-project --python 3.11 --with bpy==4.3.0 --with 'numpy<2' python penn-station-1935/model/build_context.py --out /private/tmp/penn-phase2-context --render
```

Default output is outside the live app: `context-neighbourhood.blend`, raw `context-neighbourhood.glb`, `context-manifest.json`, original 1024² PBR maps, and three visual review PNGs. The script does not edit `assets/` or the main station authoring source. The render rig is added **after** the authoring source/GLB have been saved, so inspection cameras, sun and debug ground are excluded from the delivered asset.

Repeated windows, columns, vehicle material kits and lamp parts share mesh data. Blender's `export_gpu_instances` option alone did not emit an instance extension for these linked copies. Therefore apply glTF-Transform's `instance({min: 2})` during runtime optimisation and inspect the resulting `EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing` declaration. The first proof transformation made 35 batches containing 10,717 instances. Source mesh/material counts and final compressed byte sizes should be recorded after the parent's Meshopt/KTX2 pipeline, not inferred from a checkbox.

The limestone, granite, buff-brick and pavement maps are new deterministic project-created textures, not historical-photo samples. They use subtle stochastic grain and thin staggered brick mortar. No material is claimed colour-calibrated to a surviving sample. This context preserves geometric shadows and instances; it does not claim a full baked light atlas or final photographic fidelity.
