# Pennsylvania Station — architectural revision 4

This Blender reconstruction follows the architects’ drawings, Pennsylvania Railroad publications, the engineers’ 1910 record and authenticated photographs. Revision 4 repairs arch and capital-leaf winding, resolves the coffer profile as a continuous curved surface, refines the existing capital/moulding sampling and photographed five-globe candelabra, and adds the documented concourse perimeter fenestration and square-prism floor fields. The established registered roof mass, raised end landings and nine platform accesses are retained. Small fixture profiles and the exact coffer arrangement remain reconstructed. It remains an evidence-led reconstruction with the specific qualifications below, rather than a certified survey replica.

The browser consumes Blender-exported glTF geometry. Geometry Nodes author the repeated Doric colonnades; the remaining architectural surfaces and swept structural sections are authored in `build_station.py`. Static meshes are batched by material. The editable scene and review evidence belong in repository-root `authoring/penn-station-1935/`, outside the custom public staging directory. They contain public material only; a legacy root Pages configuration can also serve that folder.

## Rebuild and review

```sh
uv run --no-project --python 3.11 --with bpy==4.3.0 --with 'numpy<2' python penn-station-1935/model/build_station.py --out ~/Desktop/penn-station-review --no-context --only core
```

`--out` writes raw GLBs, fallback material maps, the compressed editable `penn-station-study.blend`, structural-contact audit and scene manifest into a review directory. It leaves delivered app assets untouched. Without it, raw geometry goes to app assets and the editable source is named `penn-station-phase3.blend` in the authoring folder by the legacy default; use `--out` and archive the reviewed revision explicitly. Raw exports must pass the parent material, lighting, compression and browser pipeline before promotion.

`--no-context` removes the superseded generic neighbourhood and outside street surfaces. The authenticated context, railway and rolling stock are separate authored assets. `--only exterior`, `waiting` or `concourse` restricts export without changing the other raw files. `--only core` exports the three architectural chunks. The obsolete representative platform is omitted by default; `--legacy-platform` explicitly restores that earlier study and must not accompany the current complete railway asset.

UV0 is `ArchitecturalUV`; the parent pipeline preserves its metre-scaled stone grain and supplies an independent lighting UV channel. Mural fields have normalized coordinates. Painted structural iron is dielectric, while exposed rail and Monel roof metal use separate metallic materials. The waiting-room/arcade marble, Botticino booth fronts, travertine walls and interior granite columns have distinct material names. The concourse’s opaque floor grid/material remains an appearance interpretation: Gibbs’s marble-floor list does not specifically name that floor. Floor coverage revision 3 reconstructs the broad visible central court with 2,488 shared reinforced-concrete panels and 248,800 square moulded-glass units. Its 45.6×79.2 m envelope retains 20 full-thickness concrete bearing cells and 3,582.72 m² of active panels, including webs. Perimeter/cloister circulation and the east stair zone remain opaque. Plan and photograph registration establish the architectural scope; precise panel edges are inferred. Gibbs distinguishes this interior concrete-web construction from the exterior round/lead-ring type. Panel extent, 120 mm pitch, 1.2 m module, glass thickness, colour and optical response are reconstructed, rather than measured historic samples. `concourse_details.py` authors these fields and the perimeter layer; `coffer_geometry.py` resolves the existing plaster profile with constrained shared-edge triangulation.

`capture_reflections.py` normally captures only the waiting room, concourse and platform. The explicit `--only exterior` mode adds a separate elevated Seventh Avenue probe, default world position `[142,12,0]`, under the same neutral dome and afternoon reference sun. Its output/report belong in a separate review folder; the default three-room report is unchanged. These static panoramas show the authored geometry and limited context, not a historical panoramic photograph or parallax-correct reflections. The superseded modern Poly Haven environment is retained in the authoring archive for provenance.

`denoise_lighting.py INPUT --input-transfer srgb --out SCRATCH --zone waiting-room --radiance-scale 1` filters a diffuse atlas with Blender's color-only OpenImageDenoise compositor. Run through `uv` with `bpy==4.3.0`, `numpy<2` and `pillow==13.0.0`. Existing retained HDRs require `srgb` because their original save applied display transfer; corrected linear HDR/EXR inputs use `--input-transfer linear`. The helper preserves source files, writes explicitly linear RGBE HDR and once-encoded Standard/sRGB PNG/lossless WebP at full and512px sizes, and records mean/range changes, clipping and a0.18 transfer check. Exactly uniform inputs bypass the learned filter to prevent false structure. It changes neither geometry nor AO. Color-only atlas filtering can soften tiny shadow features near islands and must be reviewed on actual mapped geometry.

## Coordinates and circulation

Units are metres. Blender uses X east, Y north and Z up. glTF uses X east, Y up and Z south.

The editable `.blend` is saved after raw export with the room collections lowered 4.2672m. All collections open in one coherent world. Scene property `main_floor_world` records −4.2672; do not translate those collections again when rendering the saved scene. Manifest walkable bounds, stair endpoints and floor heights are explicitly WORLD coordinates. Architectural dimensions remain relative to Main = 0.

The working street/main difference is 14ft, reconciling street E334 with a later floor drawing near E320. Small floor gradients and exact Seventh Avenue curb registration remain qualified. Main waiting room and departure concourse share a level. The east grand stair is 39ft6in wide; its 28 six-inch risers and 300mm treads reconstruct the working drop rather than claim an authenticated original step count. Threshold slabs connect the street arcade, loggia, stair, hall and west axial passage.

The north/south entrances have raised street-level bridges and Ionic-screen landings, as the original passenger description and photographs show. The central descending stair footprint is traced from plate 300 at approximately 35ft run ×18ft clear width, with roughly ±2ft raster uncertainty. The room/clear-floor hierarchy registers each flight at |Z|31.5468…42.2148m, X ±2.7432m. Upper landings run |Z|42.2148…47.9044m, floor world 0; pedestrian bridges continue to |Z|65.532m. The end doors are physically closed. Their room-side planes are Z ±47.3244m, with the end masonry inside face at ±47.4044m. Manifest `waitingEndStairs` and `waitingRoomBounds` expose these controls for browser tests.

Exit is provisionally 10ft6in below Main, qualified against a later plan’s circa 10ft difference. Platform height is independently anchored: Gibbs specifies New York platform edges 3ft10in above rail. With rail E290ft10⅛in and Main E320, rail is −8.886825m and platform −7.718425m relative to Main. Their world heights are −13.154025m and −11.985625m. Conflicting lift travel does not determine the platform elevation.

Nine main-to-exit flights and lower doglegs register to the first nine platform rows. Their unequal spacing follows the railway agent’s heavy-coping-edge trace, checked against plan 243; two southern heads lie beyond the 320ft glazed court. The floor extends to the documented 475ft departure-concourse width. Each lower landing fits its platform polygon, rather than an invented uniform bay. The exit slab underside is world −7.7676m and receives the railway asset’s 22 platform-light mounting points.

## Attachment and route verification

The authoring script writes `architecture-contact-audit.json` before batching. It checks actual vertices at recorded bearing interfaces against supporting surfaces, with a 25mm geometric tolerance. Revision4’s immutable full-core authoring pass recorded 2,490 interface checks with no failures, covering colonnade bearings, Ionic screens, colossal orders, vault/rib supports and concourse structural joints. This is a scoped connection audit, not certification of every stone joint or a structural engineering analysis.

An independent assembled audit registers the saved world scene with the final railway asset, then casts actual geometry rays through the east–west route, all nine access flights, both end stairs, platform receiving landings and platform-light attachments. Browser collision and navigation tests are separate because permission bounds, loading and runtime collision can fail even when source geometry connects. Multi-angle exterior and low/high interior review images are retained with the authoring evidence.

## Remaining specific limits

- The143ft/150ft finished-ceiling statements conflict. The model chooses143ft explicitly; both original statements remain visible in the record.
- Exact coffer count, relief depth, moulding profiles and capital carving are interpretations of dimensioned orders and photographs, not fragment scans.
- Five mural fields remain neutral. The north field remains the native 728×260 monochrome photographic recovery from the 5076×4061 LOC scan, not a colour restoration or newly recovered higher-resolution artwork.
- The concourse perimeter now has 34 reconstructed bays: 11 west, seven north, seven south and nine east, with rectangular lower grids, separate arched heads, opaque spandrels and upper clerestories. Photographs establish this composition; precise minor sash dimensions, counts within a bay, and finishes remain photographic interpretations. The east wall preserves the physical axial passage.
- Individual roof fabrication splices, purlin/frame counts, rivet pitch and minor glazing profiles are not exhaustively certified. The N–S roof arch-foot registration is inferred; the truncated E356ft5 baseline is not independently verified as its spring.
- Main-floor gradients, exact curb registration, end-portico longitudinal placement and the provisional exit datum retain their stated uncertainty. Stair step counts reconstruct working levels and plan footprints.
- Some offices and adjoining accommodation have a closed exterior shell rather than furnished interiors. Their roof masses and real light courts are preserved; unverified shop interiors are not filled with invented period scenes.
- Opening-era ticket/parcel/telephone functions, refined booth grilles and base vents, twelve slatted benches, eight photographed five-globe standards and unassigned gate cards do not establish a complete1935 inventory. Fine carving, small furnishing profiles/placement, figurative sculpture, most mural imagery and exact period signage remain incomplete. North/south indicator numerals now face outward; exact1935 operational cards are not invented.
- The railway plan is opening-era evidence. Raster registration, narrow-platform narrative conflicts, end contours, tunnel transitions and1935 electrical sectioning have their separate railway qualifications.

The reconstruction should be assessed against matching-view primary photographs and the actual browser experience. Render polish and a working route do not establish a1:1 historical replica.

The [30 October platform-stair layout review](platform-stair-audit.md) supersedes any implication that the current upper departure-flight direction has been authenticated. The original plan and east-wall photograph place the gates on the court-facing ends; the current upper flights run in the opposite direction. Correct their orientation, registered wall margins and connecting circulation in the next iteration. Existing point-support checks do not establish historical placement.
