An evidence-led reconstruction

The building. The evidence.
The gaps between.

The original Pennsylvania Station was designed by McKim, Mead & White and opened in 1910. This evolving study uses its surviving plans and photographs to recover a sense of the architecture. It is not a surveyed replica or a completed photorealistic reconstruction.

Documented

Dimensions and structure

The nominal 784 × 430 ft envelope and 277 × 103 ft clear waiting hall anchor the model. The engineering papers give conflicting 143 ft and 150 ft ceiling heights. This reconstruction adopts the 143 ft engineering ceiling datum; the discrepancy remains documented. The roof is recorded at 167 ft above the main floor. Engineering records describe the concourse’s unequal vault spans and latticed steel piers.

The working main floor is 14 ft below the 33rd Street datum. Local slopes and differences between published dimensions remain recorded in the research ledger.

Reconstructed

Architecture, material and light

Deep octagonal coffers, five-light thermal windows and column details follow period drawings and photographs. Stone texture, colour, wear and furniture placement remain interpretations. Static sky illumination and contact shading are baked in Blender; sunlight is adjustable.

Hotel Pennsylvania, Farley and the period vehicles combine documented anchors with explicitly approximate detail. The date 1935 describes the intended period, rather than a verified inventory for that year.

Still to come

Towards the finished reconstruction

Five Guérin mural images, surveyed sculpture, complete period furnishings, photorealistic passengers and the wider Manhattan streetscape remain in development. The north mural uses a rectified monochrome archival image; its original colours remain unverified.
