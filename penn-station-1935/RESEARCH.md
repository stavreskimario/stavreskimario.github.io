# Pennsylvania Station: evidence and reconstruction register

Research date: 29 October 2026. Historical target: 1935–1940. **The target period has not yet been validated throughout the model.** The principal dimensional evidence records the original station around its opening, while several photographs establish later conditions. This is a phased architectural reconstruction study, not a surveyed replica or completed photorealistic reconstruction.

## Primary evidence

**S1 — Pennsylvania Railroad, _The New York Improvement and Tunnel Extension of the Pennsylvania Railroad_, October 1910.** [Digitized primary publication](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4f/The_New_York_improvement_and_tunnel_extension_of_the_Pennsylvania_railroad._Issued_October%2C_1910_%28IA_newyorkimproveme00penn%29.pdf). Printed page numbers below differ from PDF page indices. Printed pp. 8–12 describe the exterior; pp. 13–16 circulation, stone and the concourse; p. 18 gives a second, conflicting building-size summary. This publication is a historical record, not an as-built survey. Its promotional superlatives should not be repeated as present-day judgments.

**S2 — McKim, Mead & White, _A Monograph of the Work of McKim, Mead & White, 1879–1915_, volume IV.** [Architects' plates, Getty Research Institute scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b3/A_monograph_of_the_work_of_McKim%2C_Mead_%26_White%2C_1879-1915_.._%28IA_gri_33125006526038%29.pdf); [catalog and rights record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:A_monograph_of_the_work_of_McKim,_Mead_%26_White,_1879-1915_.._(IA_gri_33125006526038).pdf). Plate 300 supplies the block plan; 301 the Seventh Avenue elevation; 303 the exterior photograph; 306 the waiting room; 307 the restaurant and concourse; 308 waiting-room details; 309 the exterior order; 310 interior details. Publication dating varies between catalog records; the original monograph was published before 1931. A caption's “1906–1910” dates the building, not necessarily the photographic exposure.

**S3 — Historic American Buildings Survey, NY-5471, Pennsylvania Station.** [LOC catalog](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/ny0411/); [seven-page historical report](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/ny/ny0400/ny0411/data/ny0411data.pdf). The report identifies the station's site and architects, describes the separate circulation levels, identifies original suppliers, and points to structural drawings in George Gibbs's ASCE paper, _Transactions_ LXIX (1910), pp. 226–383. This is an authoritative historical inventory; it does not provide a complete measured drawing set.

**S4 — NYC Municipal Archives, Department of Buildings and WPA Federal Writers' Project collections.** [Archive article with original elevations and 1936 photographs](https://www.archives.nyc/blog/2024/6/7/re-discovering-the-old-pennsylvania-station). It reproduces Seventh and Eighth Avenue elevations, an exterior photograph dated approximately 1936, and an interior photographed on 13 March 1936 by Ezzes. It also identifies a 1926 alteration of the LIRR passage to the waiting room. The archive states that the surviving new-building plans are incomplete. These materials are linked for research; copies have not been bundled because item-level reuse terms were not established.

**S5 — NYPL, Berenice Abbott, _Penn Station, Interior, Manhattan_.** [NYPL item](https://digitalcollections.nypl.org/items/510d47d9-4f0c-a3d9-e040-e00a18064a99); [Changing New York collection](https://old-digitalcollections.nypl.org/collections/changing-new-york?filters%5Bname%5D=Pennsylvania+Station+%28New+York%2C+N.Y.%29&keywords=). Collection dating spans 1935–1938, with later prints also held. For a precisely dated comparison, the [National Gallery of Art's Abbott photograph](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/179830-pennsylvania-station) is dated 14 July 1936. The NGA item expressly makes its media unavailable for download and requests an image-usage inquiry. Neither photograph is bundled here; do not assume that every Abbott print shares the same rights status.

**S6 — Pennsylvania Railroad, _Pennsylvania Station in New York City_, 1910 booklet (LOC catalog).** [LOC scan](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/public/gdcmassbookdig/pennsylvaniastat00penn/pennsylvaniastat00penn.pdf). PDF pages 17, 19, 25–27 and 39 (one based) record overall dimensions, the arcade, waiting room, concourse/platform arrangement and plan. This is an additional primary account, useful for documenting discrepancies rather than silently selecting whichever dimension is convenient.

**S7 — Sanborn Map Company, Manhattan volume 2 (1920), LOC.** [Catalog and map sheets](https://www.loc.gov/item/sanborn06116_048/). LOC expressly describes its online Sanborn collection as public domain and free to reuse. Relevant sheet coverage and later revisions must be checked before deriving the 1935–1940 neighbourhood from this volume. No neighbouring building has been certified from this map in this phase.

**S8 — _Real Estate Record and Builders' Guide_, 20 August 1910, “Structural Wonders of the Penn. Station.”** [Period journal scan, Columbia University](https://rerecord.library.columbia.edu/pdf_files/ldpd_7031148_046_8.pdf). The article distinguishes the eight giant granite Corinthian columns from the travertine of other walls, pilasters and stairs. This is more specific than S1's general description of travertine surfaces; historical finish specifications and later paint remain subjects for review.

## Plan interpretation used for layout

S2 plate 300 was inspected visually after rendering its scan. In the printed plate Seventh Avenue is at the bottom, Eighth Avenue at the top, 31st Street at the left and 33rd Street at the right. The arcade, loggia, main waiting room, connecting passage and concourse share the east–west central axis. The two subsidiary waiting rooms sit west of the general waiting room, one on either side of the central passage. The carriage courts flank the eastern arcade and dining/loggia group.

Approximate measurements read from this small raster plan put the waiting-room centre about 402 ft / 122.5 m west of the Seventh Avenue facade and the concourse centre about 632 ft / 193 m west. These are **raster-derived estimates**, approximately ±5 ft before reconciling the building-width discrepancy. They are useful to prevent reversing the rooms or collapsing their spacing; they are not construction coordinates.

## Explicit reconstruction and uncertainty list

**Verified from the historical evidence:** site orientation and blocked-through West 32nd Street; sequence of primary rooms; difference between exterior Doric and interior Corinthian orders; major published dimensions (including their conflicts); plate 310's interior column, entablature and lower arch dimensions; three side lunettes and one end lunette; exposed intersecting steel/glass concourse vaults; separation of circulation levels. The 33 ft 4 in roof-lunette radius was checked against the actual page image of S1 printed p. 12, not just OCR or a modern transcription.

**Reconstruction choices requiring review:** individual facade bay spacing, entasis and moulding profiles where not traced; interpretation of clear versus shell waiting-room dimensions; exact location of stairs and floor levels; stone colour and texture scale; glazing thickness, transmission and soiling; joinery, clocks, lighting and railings; unfinished or simplified sculpture.

**Unverified for 1935–1940:** changes made since 1910 (including 1926 LIRR alterations); precise ticket counter/sign positions; train types and consists; paint and metal patina; every neighbouring parcel, rooftop and storefront; road surfacing and markings; streetlights, traffic signals, taxis and pedestrian clothing. Context masses must be labelled as context studies until mapped individually. No invented shop sign should be presented as a documented business.

**Visual accuracy not yet established:** photographic camera alignment, ornament silhouette/placement at close range, calibrated material response, reference sun azimuth, indoor exposure, GI bake accuracy and the effect of later maintenance. Rendering at high resolution cannot substitute for those checks.

## Phased delivery and acceptance gates

1. **Evidence-led architectural study (current phase):** provide an explorable authored model, the room sequence and source ledger, an exhibition interface, explicit uncertainty labels, and authentic reference images. This phase cannot be described as a complete photorealistic 1935 reconstruction.
2. **Measured architectural asset:** trace and reconcile plans, sections and elevations; model the complete eastern entrance route, exact order/mouldings, grand stairs and three waiting-room vaults; author and review the concourse intersection topology, platform openings and ornamental metalwork. Accept only after reference silhouettes and measured control points agree.
3. **Photorealistic vertical slice:** choose one documented exterior angle and one waiting-room/concourse angle; create UVs, high-resolution licensed PBR maps, normal detail, calibrated glazing and exposure; bake GI and validate against photographs. Keep Blender source, bake settings and LODs reproducible. Ship compressed runtime assets only after inspecting the compressed result.
4. **Circa-1936 city and operation:** reconstruct immediately adjacent parcels from date-checked maps and photographs; verify vehicle/rail stock and signage; add collision meshes, navigable level changes and people with sourced clothing. Expand outward only after the station passes architectural review.
5. **Release candidate:** compare matching cameras, record dimensional checks and uncertain features, verify desktop/mobile controls and performance, test every relative asset path on the GitHub Pages build, and record actual device/browser results. A pull request may be opened for review; merge requires the owner's authorisation.

The final quality gate is the user's requested photographic and historical standard, not the existence of a functioning 3 D viewport. Missing bakes, compressed textures, detailed urban assets or verified ornament must remain visible in the delivery limitations.


## Phase 2: original engineering structure and floor levels

**S9 — George B. Francis and Joseph H. O’Brien, “Certain Engineering Structures of the New York Terminal Area,” ASCE Transactions LXIX, paper 1164, October 1910.** [Official DOI](https://ascelibrary.org/doi/10.1061/TACEAT.0002221); [original volume scan](https://archive.org/download/transactionsofam69amer/transactionsofam69amer.pdf). Printed pp. 188–200 and 204 are critical. Plates LXIX and LXX are PDF pages 345 and 349 (one based); use their fronts, since their backs contain faint mirrored lines. This original 1910 US publication is public domain in the United States. New scan extracts remain in research scratch storage.

**S10 — George Gibbs, “Station Construction, Road, Track, Yard Equipment, Electric Traction, and Locomotives,” same volume, paper 1165.** [Official DOI](https://ascelibrary.org/doi/10.1061/TACEAT.0002223); same original scan. Printed pp. 235,240–252,261–262 establish public and railway circulation. In the Archive PDF, page 405 is the track plan/cross-section;425 the street plan;427 the waiting-level plan;428 the exit-level plan.

### Concourse roof

Three barrels run east–west: outer spans 85 ft 10 in, middle 95 ft 4 in. A 98 ft-span barrel runs north–south through them. Two 27 ft 8 in bays lie west of the intersection; one 26 ft 8 in bay lies east. Plate LXX gives 320 ft north–south overall, including 26 ft 6 in lower-vault borders on both sides. Its east–west chain is 29 ft 5 in +27 ft 8 in +27 ft 8 in +98 ft +26 ft 8 in +1 ft 6 in =210 ft 11 in. These are frame dimensions, distinct from the rounded 340×210 ft court description.

The 3 ft-square piers comprise four 8×8 in angles with double lattice on all four faces. Arched trusses have paired webs about 2 ft apart, changing depth between crown and haunch. Diagonal groin trusses project above the roof as monitors. Purlins and glazing ribs are secondary members. Lower Guastavino/steel vaults border the north,south andwest; the main roof abuts the subsidiary waiting rooms on the east.

Plan 243 visibly shows nine stairheads along the east edge and a different arrangement of track openings and circulation to the west. Complete platform-number mapping has not been certified. The 475 ft-wide departure floor extends under streets beyond the glazed courtyard. Eleven platforms do not justify eleven identical holes in the court.

### Supported floor registration and its limits

S10 pp. 240–244 places the arcade at street level, the waiting room one storey below, and the subsidiary waiting rooms and departure concourse on that same lower main level. The grand stair descends from arcade to hall. The exit concourse lies below the departure concourse and above platforms.

Two drawing labels anchor the working model: **S2 plate 302 labels the 33rd Street reference “ELEVATION 334.000”; S4’s original 1926 Gibbs & Hill drawingD-4088/2 marks the waiting-room floor approximatelyE 320 ft.** The latter left label reads 320 ft 0¾in; a new passage floor is 319 ft 11 in. The adjacent floor varies slightly, and the right label’s fractional inches remain uncertified at the supplied resolution. The [Municipal Archives reproduces the original drawing](https://www.archives.nyc/blog/2024/6/7/re-discovering-the-old-pennsylvania-station) under “Passage L.I. Concourse to Main Waiting Room.” It is linked for research only; no copy is bundled.

A **14 ft/4.2672 m street-to-main change** is thus a supported working registration, with small curb/floor gradients unresolved. There is an independent consistency check: S9’s 167 ft from waiting floor to highest roof plusE 320 equalsE 487; the 153 ft from street to highest roof in PRR summaries plusE 334 also equalsE 487. This does not certify every curb, particularly the Seventh Avenue entrance spot, or the riser count of each staircase.

**Actual New York platform height is 3 ft 10 in/1.1684 m above top of rail.** S10 p. 261 explicitly specifies this for the eleven platforms under the station; plate LXXVIII independently dimensions the cross-section. The platform edge is 5 ft 3 in/1.6002 m from track centre. Applying this to the 1926 drawing’s local railE 290 ft 10⅛in gives platformE 294 ft 8⅛in and a mainE 320-to-platform drop 25 ft 3⅞in/7.718425 m. The rail-to-main difference is 29 ft 1⅞in/8.886825 m. These are derived local values, not a claim that every graded track has the identical elevation.

S10 p. 262 also lists some passenger lifts travelling 27 ft 6 in from platform to main concourse and 17 ft to the exit concourse. S9 describes waiting floor about 27 ft above top of rail; S1 gives 18 ft exit height. These conflicting rounded/local accounts cannot override the explicit platform section or be indiscriminately combined into one universal stack. The model should use the documented high platform, identify its adopted rail registration and keep lift/destination differences in the uncertainty list.

Plate LXX’s concourse crown isE 417 ft 6⅜in. Its truncated bottomE 356 ft 5 in lies around third-floor framing; **it is not the public floor**. Subtracting workingE 320 gives 97 ft 6⅜in/29.727925 m above the main floor, cohering with the approximately 100 ft narrative height. ASCE’s 300 ft offset is referenced around mean high water, not a modern vertical datum.

### Waiting-room measurement hierarchy and fenestration

S9 p. 188 expressly calls the lantern’s external dimensions 325×119 ft; p. 194 gives the structural frame 320×112 ft 10 in at column centres. S1 p. 12 reports room 314 ft 4 in×108 ft 8 in; S6 gives 277×103 ft. The width hierarchy is exterior 119 ft/36.2712 m; frame 112 ft 10 in/34.3916 m; reported room 108 ft 8 in/33.1216 m; clear 103 ft/31.3944 m. Therefore 108 ft 8 in is not a verified outer shell. Finished wall, pilaster and Ionic-screen control planes remain to be reconciled.

Plate 302 shows **five tall primary glazing divisions within each lunette**, separated by four substantial vertical mullions. Three refers to the number of lunettes per long wall. Its longwall section distinguishes the central lower arched passage from large rectangular flanking panels. Repeating three identical lower arches would be incorrect.

Plate 302 labels the horizontal line through the thermal-window sill region and centres of the circular features at the window bases **ELEVATION 421.25** on both sides. Relative to the working main-floor datum E320, that line is 101 ft 3 in (30.861 m) above the main floor; the drawing scale corroborates its 65 ft 9 in distance below roof E487. This verifies the labelled line. The small offset between that line, clear-glazing bottom and individual sill mouldings must still be resolved before treating 30.861 m as an exact glazing-base dimension. The 31 m window-base value remains a close inference. This section alone does not establish the identity or dimensions of all circular features as operational clocks.

S10 printed p. 267 describes four exterior clocks of 6 ft diameter, fourteen public-room clocks ranging from 18 in to 15 ft, and one motor-driven 15 ft dial in the main waiting room. This conflicts with S1's 7 ft exterior-dial description; their exact drawing/installation dates and dial-versus-case extents have not been reconciled. The singular 15 ft waiting-room instrument does not establish six identical clocks at all window bases. Clock locations, cases and changes by 1935 require photograph matching.

### Waiting-room ceiling height remains provisional

Francis and O’Brien (S9, printed p. 194) describe 143 ft from the waiting-room floor to the highest ceiling and 167 ft to the highest roof. Gibbs (S10, table p. 252 and construction account p. 257) gives 150 ft; p. 257 explicitly calls it floor to ceiling. The PRR booklet reports a 150 ft room height without resolving the datum; S6 uses floor-to-roof wording. These accounts conflict. Phase 3 adopts the explicit 143 ft highest-ceiling datum, retains the 167 ft roof and records Gibbs's contrary 150 ft statement. This modelling choice does not certify that the source discrepancy is resolved. S9 also calls the floor approximately 27 ft above rail, another local/dated datum that should not silently replace the independently registered 1926 rail level.
