# Pennsylvania Station: evidence and reconstruction register

Research date: 29 October 2026. Historical target: 1935–1940. **The target period has not yet been validated throughout the model.** The principal dimensional evidence records the original station around its opening, while several photographs establish later conditions. This is a phased architectural reconstruction study, not a surveyed replica or completed photorealistic reconstruction.

## Primary evidence

**S1 — Pennsylvania Railroad, _The New York Improvement and Tunnel Extension of the Pennsylvania Railroad_, October 1910.** [Digitized primary publication](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4f/The_New_York_improvement_and_tunnel_extension_of_the_Pennsylvania_railroad._Issued_October%2C_1910_%28IA_newyorkimproveme00penn%29.pdf). Printed page numbers below differ from PDF page indices. Printed pp. 8–12 describe the exterior; pp. 13–16 circulation, stone and the concourse; p. 18 gives a second, conflicting building-size summary. This publication is a historical record, not an as-built survey. Its promotional superlatives should not be repeated as present-day judgments.

**S2 — McKim, Mead & White, _A Monograph of the Work of McKim, Mead & White, 1879–1915_, volume IV.** [Architects' plates, Getty Research Institute scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b3/A_monograph_of_the_work_of_McKim%2C_Mead_%26_White%2C_1879-1915_.._%28IA_gri_33125006526038%29.pdf); [academic-library copy](https://resources.library.nd.edu/documents/architecture/McKMW-Monograph-v4.pdf); [catalog and rights record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:A_monograph_of_the_work_of_McKim,_Mead_%26_White,_1879-1915_.._(IA_gri_33125006526038).pdf). Plate 300 supplies the block plan; 301 the Seventh Avenue elevation; 303 the exterior photograph; 306 the waiting room; 307 the restaurant and concourse; 308 waiting-room details; 309 the exterior order; 310 interior details. Publication dating varies between catalog records; the original monograph was published before 1931. A caption's “1906–1910” dates the building, not necessarily the photographic exposure.

**S3 — Historic American Buildings Survey, NY-5471, Pennsylvania Station.** [LOC catalog](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/ny0411/); [seven-page historical report](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/ny/ny0400/ny0411/data/ny0411data.pdf). The report identifies the station's site and architects, describes the separate circulation levels, identifies original suppliers, and points to structural drawings in George Gibbs's ASCE paper, _Transactions_ LXIX (1910), pp. 226–383. This is an authoritative historical inventory; it does not provide a complete measured drawing set.

**S4 — NYC Municipal Archives, Department of Buildings and WPA Federal Writers' Project collections.** [Archive article with original elevations and 1936 photographs](https://www.archives.nyc/blog/2024/6/7/re-discovering-the-old-pennsylvania-station). It reproduces Seventh and Eighth Avenue elevations, an exterior photograph dated approximately 1936, and an interior photographed on 13 March 1936 by Ezzes. It also identifies a 1926 alteration of the LIRR passage to the waiting room. The archive states that the surviving new-building plans are incomplete. These materials are linked for research; copies have not been bundled because item-level reuse terms were not established.

**S5 — NYPL, Berenice Abbott, _Penn Station, Interior, Manhattan_.** [NYPL item](https://digitalcollections.nypl.org/items/510d47d9-4f0c-a3d9-e040-e00a18064a99); [Changing New York collection](https://old-digitalcollections.nypl.org/collections/changing-new-york?filters%5Bname%5D=Pennsylvania+Station+%28New+York%2C+N.Y.%29&keywords=). Collection dating spans 1935–1938, with later prints also held. For a precisely dated comparison, the [National Gallery of Art's Abbott photograph](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/179830-pennsylvania-station) is dated 14 July 1936. The NGA item expressly makes its media unavailable for download and requests an image-usage inquiry. Neither photograph is bundled here; do not assume that every Abbott print shares the same rights status.

**S6 — Pennsylvania Railroad, _Pennsylvania Station in New York City_, 1909-era booklet.** [LOC scan](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/public/gdcmassbookdig/pennsylvaniastat00penn/pennsylvaniastat00penn.pdf). PDF pages 17, 19, 25–27 and 39 (one based) record overall dimensions, the arcade, waiting room, concourse/platform arrangement and plan. This is an additional primary account, useful for documenting discrepancies rather than silently selecting whichever dimension is convenient.

**S7 — Sanborn Map Company, Manhattan volume 2 (1920), LOC.** [Catalog and map sheets](https://www.loc.gov/item/sanborn06116_048/). LOC expressly describes its online Sanborn collection as public domain and free to reuse. Relevant sheet coverage and later revisions must be checked before deriving the 1935–1940 neighbourhood from this volume. No neighbouring building has been certified from this map in this phase.

**S8 — _Real Estate Record and Builders' Guide_, 20 August 1910, “Structural Wonders of the Penn. Station.”** [Period journal scan, Columbia University](https://rerecord.library.columbia.edu/pdf_files/ldpd_7031148_046_8.pdf). The article distinguishes the eight giant granite Corinthian columns from the travertine of other walls, pilasters and stairs. This is more specific than S1's general description of travertine surfaces; historical finish specifications and later paint remain subjects for review.

## Plan interpretation used for layout

S2 plate 300 was inspected visually after rendering its scan. In the printed plate Seventh Avenue is at the bottom, Eighth Avenue at the top, 31st Street at the left and 33rd Street at the right. The arcade, loggia, main waiting room, connecting passage and concourse share the east–west central axis. The two subsidiary waiting rooms sit west of the general waiting room, one on either side of the central passage. The carriage courts flank the eastern arcade and dining/loggia group.

Approximate measurements read from this small raster plan put the waiting-room centre about 402 ft / 122.5 m west of the Seventh Avenue facade and the concourse centre about 632 ft / 193 m west. These are **raster-derived estimates**, approximately ±5 ft before reconciling the building-width discrepancy. They are useful to prevent reversing the rooms or collapsing their spacing; they are not construction coordinates.

## Explicit reconstruction and uncertainty list

**Verified from the historical evidence:** site orientation and blocked-through West 32nd Street; sequence of primary rooms; difference between exterior Doric and interior Corinthian orders; major published dimensions (including their conflicts); plate 310's interior column, entablature and lower arch dimensions; three side lunettes and one end lunette; exposed intersecting steel/glass concourse vaults; separation of circulation levels. The 33 ft 4 in roof-lunette radius was checked against the actual page image of S1 printed p. 12, not just OCR or a modern transcription.

**Reconstruction choices requiring review:** individual facade bay spacing, entasis and moulding profiles where not traced; interpretation of clear versus shell waiting-room dimensions; exact location of stairs and floor levels; stone colour and texture scale; glazing thickness, transmission and soiling; joinery, clocks, lighting and railings; unfinished or simplified sculpture.

**Unverified for 1935–1940:** changes made since 1910 (including 1926 LIRR alterations); precise ticket counter/sign positions; train types and consists; paint and metal patina; every neighbouring parcel, rooftop and storefront; road surfacing and markings; streetlights, traffic signals, taxis and pedestrian clothing. Context masses must be labelled as context studies until mapped individually. No invented shop sign should be presented as a documented business.

**Visual accuracy not yet established:** photographic camera alignment, ornament silhouette/placement at close range, calibrated material response, reference sun azimuth, indoor exposure, GI bake accuracy and the effect of later maintenance. Rendering at high resolution cannot substitute for those checks.

## Phased delivery and acceptance gates

1. **Evidence-led architectural study (current phase):** provide an explorable authored model, the room sequence and source ledger, an exhibition interface, explicit uncertainty labels, and authentic reference images. This phase cannot be described as a complete photorealistic 1935 reconstruction.
2. **Measured architectural asset:** trace and reconcile plans, sections and elevations; model the complete eastern entrance route, exact order/mouldings, grand stairs and three waiting-room vaults; author and review the concourse intersection topology, platform openings and ornamental metalwork. Accept only after reference silhouettes and measured control points agree.
3. **Photorealistic vertical slice:** choose one documented exterior angle and one waiting-room/concourse angle; create UVs, high-resolution licensed PBR maps, normal detail, calibrated glazing and exposure; bake GI and validate against photographs. Keep Blender source, bake settings and LODs reproducible. Ship compressed runtime assets only after inspecting the compressed result.
4. **Circa-1936 city and operation:** reconstruct immediately adjacent parcels from date-checked maps and photographs; verify vehicle/rail stock and signage; add collision meshes, navigable level changes and people with sourced clothing. Expand outward only after the station passes architectural review.
5. **Release candidate:** compare matching cameras, record dimensional checks and uncertain features, verify desktop/mobile controls and performance, test every relative asset path on the GitHub Pages build, and record actual device/browser results. A pull request may be opened for review; merge requires the owner's authorisation.

The final quality gate is the user's requested photographic and historical standard, not the existence of a functioning 3D viewport. Missing bakes, compressed textures, detailed urban assets or verified ornament must remain visible in the delivery limitations.
