The palace in the photograph

Glorious iron. Enormous glass.

Following the Great Exhibition in Hyde Park, Joseph Paxton's building was substantially redesigned and enlarged at Sydenham, reopening in 1854. Unlike the 1851 structure, the Sydenham palace had a long barrel-vaulted nave, a monumental central transept, two smaller end transepts and elaborate terraces, fountains and water towers.

The northern transept was lost in the 1866 fire and was never rebuilt. This visualization represents the later photographic-era composition with the central and southern transepts, not the complete 1854 plan. The entire building was destroyed by fire in 1936.

View archival reference and model accuracy

Historically scaled does not mean an exact replica. The documented main dimensions and major roof forms guide the geometry.
