An impossible building, made possible

Built in glass. Remembered in light.

For the Great Exhibition of 1851, Joseph Paxton designed an immense structure of repeatable prefabricated iron and glass elements. The original Hyde Park building had stepped, ridge-and-furrow roofs and a single great cross-vault, rather than the sweeping nave and multiple transepts of its later Sydenham incarnation.

The Hyde Park structure was dismantled after the Exhibition. It was rebuilt, enlarged and substantially redesigned at Sydenham in 1854; that later palace was destroyed by fire in 1936.

Research sources & model accuracy

This is an interpretive, browser-rendered model, not a measured archaeological reconstruction. Overall dimensions, the 24-foot grid, stepped roof heights, transept and north projection are based on historical plans and descriptions. Individual glass panes, mullion counts, ornamental details and trees are simplified for legibility and performance.
