# Independent source photograph comparison and historical limits

This review compares actual architectural source renders with the original Detroit photographs and the main-floor plan. It is not final browser, lighting, performance or complete historical-fidelity acceptance. The model images are rendered geometry; neither image generation nor photographic inpainting was used.

## Evidence and image identity

- **Concourse:** [LOC 2016815390, Detroit negative 071935 / det.4a23718](https://www.loc.gov/item/2016815390/) against the [revision 3 actual Blender-source view](../concourse-details/floor-v3/source-preview/concourse-floor-v3-source-preview.png). The unedited historical overview and item-specific rights are in [references/phase4](../../../references/phase4/). The model camera approximates the architectural view; it is not a solved photographic camera. Its [settings and source-scene hash](../concourse-details/floor-v3/source-preview/preview.json) identify the render independently of the final browser lighting.
- **Waiting room:** [LOC 2016815386, Detroit negative 071928 / det.4a23713](https://www.loc.gov/item/2016815386/) against the [actual source preview](source-preview/waiting-room-daylight.png). The [render record](source-preview/source-render.json) identifies its earlier whole-core input. The later floor-only concourse revision does not change waiting-room geometry. The model image and original photograph have different framing and exposure; this is a qualitative architectural comparison, not a pixel registration.
- **Plan:** [Gibbs, ASCE volume 69](https://archive.org/details/transactionsofam69amer), Fig. 2, printed p. 243 / scan PDF page 427. It registers the waiting room, concourse, cloisters and access heads. It does not provide a glass-panel fabrication or exact panel-edge schedule.
- **Late-period check:** the [Municipal Archives' dated 13 March 1936 west-wall photograph](https://www.archives.nyc/blog/2024/6/7/re-discovering-the-old-pennsylvania-station) supports separate rectangular lower sash bodies, arched heads and opaque masonry. It also shows a clock and raised doorway whose exact model registration remains unresolved. That image stays link-only; it is not redistributed in this review.

## Comparison findings

| Feature | What primary evidence supports | Current result and remaining gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concourse overall structure | Intersecting glass barrels, lattice iron piers, lower ornamental arches and an enclosed masonry perimeter | The model conveys these large architectural relationships. Small iron sections, joints, rivet spacing and glazing bars remain reconstructed; finer repetition alone does not certify them. The approximate camera currently shows more of the west cloister soffit than the historical photo, so apparent screen-space differences must not be treated as surveyed dimension errors. |
| Concourse ticket wall | A dominant arched opening, smaller flanking openings, lower office fronts and the ticket/baggage sign | The restored wall supplies that hierarchy and photographed functional wording while retaining an actual axial passage. Minor mouldings, rustication/course joints, fanlight tracery, sash profiles, office-front divisions and sign lettering are visibly simpler than the original. The bay counts and dimensions are interpreted, not a recovered fabrication schedule. |
| Other concourse windows | Gridded rectangular bodies separated from high arched lights by stone, corroborated in the dated 1936 west view | The corrected separation is present in the authored enclosure. Extending that vocabulary to less fully photographed elevations, along with precise heights, widths and sash counts, remains an inference. |
| Prism flooring | Broad square-grid flooring visible in negative 071935; Gibbs p. 256 explicitly distinguishes square interior glass in reinforced concrete from exterior circular units with lead rings | Revision 3 now reconstructs the broad visible central court, retaining concrete at the pier bases and circulation edges. Exact panel boundaries, 45.6×79.2 m envelope, 0.12 m pitch, 1.2 m module, mould profile, glass thickness, colour and surface wear remain inferred. This is not a survey of all 155,000 sq ft throughout the station and its surroundings. The current source image's repeated grid and reflected daylight do not certify the final browser transmission or lower-level light transport. |
| Waiting-room vault | Monumental intersecting barrels, octagonal recessed coffers and detailed framing visible in negative 071928 | Corrected continuous curved surfaces remove the former polygon/winding defects. The source preview still simplifies the many fine moulding bands, small rosettes and intermediate ornament visible in the photograph. The 341-cell arrangement and 360 mm vertical relief are interpretations. The 143 ft versus 150 ft ceiling-height source conflict remains unresolved. |
| Capitals and cornices | Rich Corinthian acanthus, layered entablatures and smaller Ionic screens | The model's order heights and supported relationships have dimensioned controls. The carved silhouette, leaf lobes, volutes, dentil/egg-and-dart detail and small joints remain visibly simplified. Smoother geometry does not establish fragment-level historical accuracy. |
| Lamps and furnishings | Five-globe floor standards, counters/grilles, vents and wood seating appear in original photographs | The raised centre, four lower branches, supported fixtures, articulated fronts and slatted benches restore the broad construction vocabulary. Exact bronze casting, finish, dimensions, full 1935 inventory and every placement remain unverified. Some photographed wall-mounted lights and small fittings remain incomplete. No generic crowd has been added to fill those gaps. |
| Murals and clocks | Six map fields are documented; one genuine north-end map can be recovered from negative 071928. Contemporary records describe clocks but do not solve every placement | The north panel retains its native 728×260 monochrome recovery; the other five remain neutral. Original palette and fine lettering are not recovered. A new main-room clock is not placed at an unsupported point. The dated concourse clock needs a separate matched-view registration; exterior 6 ft/7 ft descriptions remain conflicting. |
| Material colour and daylight | Monochrome photographs show tonal relationships and reflection, while Gibbs identifies several stone types | Current stone colours, roughness, glass optics, weather and exposure are interpretations rather than measurements from surviving samples. Source previews use reconstructed daylight. They do not reproduce a dated historical exposure, establish original colour or certify the final new baked lighting. |

The restored layers make substantial omissions and defective shading geometry reviewable. They do not yet substantiate a 1:1 replica, a complete 1935 inventory or the requested final photographic realism. The current iteration must still pass its integrated desktop-browser review after the final bakes and asset promotion. Realistic period passengers, fine ornament, five murals, unresolved clock placement and the fuller Manhattan streetscape remain explicit future work.
