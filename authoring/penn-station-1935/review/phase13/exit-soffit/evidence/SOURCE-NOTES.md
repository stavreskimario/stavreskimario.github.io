The visible gray/brown band is a material and baked-light transition across continuous existing geometry. The old atlas uses valid UV0/AO channel 0; the new atlas uses valid UV1/AO channel 1. Neither a missing roof nor missing UV1 is established. At the measured seam, both undersides are at world Y -4.707200057 m and meet at Z -72.389999390 m.

The frozen selection is **56 triangles total: 52 old concrete + 4 new travertine**. The old set spans 918.124 m² and the new set 534.034 m². They are existing large source faces: the group bounds are not infill footprints or a new survey. Node/primitive IDs belong to the recorded source snapshot. Geometry must match the oriented corners/normals/UV0 to the current assembly and fail closed if they differ.

Francis/O’Brien describe reinforced-concrete slabs and haunches. Gibbs identifies stone concrete below street. The PRR 1910 lower Exit photograph supports plain pale mineral-looking underside surfaces; its monochrome tones cannot establish their color or whether exposed concrete, paint or plaster produced the finish. Gibbs’ enamel-brick statement concerns walls, while his travertine statement concerns arcade/waiting-room interiors. Neither verifies a travertine lower passage ceiling.

Use one restrained pale mineral material and bake these existing undersides together. The proposed neutral color/roughness are rendering starting values, explicitly reconstructed. Preserve prism fields, road volume, stairs and all geometric levels. Exact local coating, original hue and 1935–1940 condition remain unresolved. Do not infer a new continuous slab from the north section’s 15ft sidewalk / 30ft roadway arrangement.

No new photograph, huge PDF, interpretation rendering or duplicate crop is included. `owned-source-faces.json` contains the exact snapshot corner data; `atlas-seam-samples.json` retains the actual measurement. This packet is a scope freeze, not evidence that the finish has already been implemented or tested in a native walking session.
