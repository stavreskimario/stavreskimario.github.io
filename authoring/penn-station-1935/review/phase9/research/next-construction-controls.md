## Recommended first bounded change

The existing platform solid prisms do not express the documented overhang and coved under-edge.

## Platform overhead enclosure

The station is a bridge over the railway, with concrete floor slabs/haunches and irregular steel framing (Francis/O'Brien pp188-191; Gibbs pp240,255-256). Glass floor lights illuminate Exit/platforms beneath selected Main areas. The station did **not** have a generic low canopy uniformly closing the whole train court: the authenticated lower photographs visibly show open upward views beside decks and stairs.

First finish actual floor/deck soffits, perimeter wall backs, frame ends and pier contacts inside source-supported building/viaduct footprints. Leave genuine open-to-roof court volumes open. Any broad new slab must have a traced footprint, a real receiving structure and appropriate stair/lift holes; an opaque full-width horizontal ceiling inserted to conceal gaps is not acceptable. Individual overhead depths/positions still need original sections/photographic matching.

## Seventh Avenue arcade and loggia controls

The user-reported street-to-waiting-room route is the **Seventh Avenue arcade**, its western loggia and the descending Main stair, not another train hall. The PRR1910 booklet [LOC10027726](https://www.loc.gov/item/10027726/) is public domain according to the item record. Cached S6 booklet PDF18 photograph and PDF19 prose show/document:

- A street-level barrel-vaulted shopping arcade, continuous upper walls and high arched side windows, with lower shopfront openings/glazing on both sides.
- An expanded western loggia, with dining/cafe and lunch/buffet entrances to its sides.
- A broad stone stair descending one floor to the waiting room, with Cassatt's niche at the side (Gibbs p249 gives40ft stair width).
- The booklet says225x45ft; Gibbs p252 instead gives220x40ft. Retain the5ft discrepancy instead of claiming a newly verified exact finished width/length.
